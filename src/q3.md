# Q3: Largest Changes in the Share of Electricity Generation from Clean Sources and How They Unfolded, 2000–2024

**Question:** Which countries gained or lost the most clean electricity share between 2000 and 2024, and how did that change unfold over the years?

This page looks at **clean sources of electricity generation**, not overall energy use. Clean means renewables plus nuclear, as a percentage of a country's domestic electricity generation. Fuels burned for transport, heating, and industry are not included. Only the 108 countries with endpoint data and at least 10 TWh of generation in 2024 are compared.

We explore the question with three plots:

1. **[Largest Increases and Decreases](#largest-increases-and-decreases)** is a pair of ranked horizontal bar charts.
2. **[Clean Shares at Start and End](#clean-shares-at-start-and-end)** is a pair of dumbbell charts.
3. **[Clean Share Over Time](#clean-share-over-time)** is a multi-line chart of selected countries.

The three plots answer the same question in different ways. They differ in what they make easy to read.

| | Largest Increases and Decreases | Clean Shares at Start and End | Clean Share Over Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shows** | The size of each change, as a bar length | Where each country started and ended, and the change between them | The clean share of every year from 2000 to 2024 for countries you choose |
| **Strengths** | The size of a change is the easiest thing to read, and bars from both panels can be compared directly on one scale. | The starting and ending levels are visible, so a country that moved from 5% to 35% is distinguishable from one that moved from 60% to 90%. | The path between the endpoints is visible: when the change happened, whether it was steady or sudden, and whether it reversed. |
| **Weaknesses** | Two countries with the same change look identical, even when one started near zero and the other was already high. The final level is not shown. | The line joins two endpoints, so everything in between is hidden. Size of change is read from a thin line, which is harder to compare than bar length. | Lines overlap and become hard to follow when many countries are selected, and the change itself is not shown as a single number. |
| **Best For** | Ranking countries by how much they changed | Judging how far each country got, and from where | Understanding how and when particular countries changed |

Reading them together gives the fullest picture: use the first plot to find the biggest movers, the second to see what those moves meant for each country's clean share, and the third to see how each country got there.

All three plots have a **Full Screen** button in their toolbar. Full screen enlarges the plot, and its controls stay available there. The Countries sliders in the three plots are linked: moving one moves the others to the same value, so you only set it once.

## Largest Increases and Decreases

Bars show each country's change in clean share of electricity generation between 2000 and 2024, in **percentage points**. The left panel shows the largest increases and the right panel the largest decreases. We chose bars because the question asks about the size of a change, and bar length is the most accurate way to compare sizes. Both panels share one symmetric scale, so a bar in one panel can be compared directly with a bar in the other. Teal marks increases and rust marks decreases.

Use the slider to change how many countries each panel shows, and hover a bar for a tooltip with the country's name, its 2000 and 2024 clean shares, the change in percentage points, and the change in fossil generation in TWh.

// Pick the Largest Increases and Decreases (Only Positive Gains and Negative Losses Appear)
function pickTails(n) {
  const increases = comparison
    .filter((d) => d.d_clean_share_pp > 0)
    .sort((a, b) => d3.descending(a.d_clean_share_pp, b.d_clean_share_pp) || d3.ascending(a.country, b.country))
    .slice(0, n);

  const decreases = comparison
    .filter((d) => d.d_clean_share_pp < 0)
    .sort((a, b) => d3.ascending(a.d_clean_share_pp, b.d_clean_share_pp) || d3.ascending(a.country, b.country))
    .slice(0, n);

  return {increases, decreases};
}

## Clean Shares at Start and End

The same countries, showing where each began and ended: an open circle marks 2000 and a filled diamond marks 2024. We chose dumbbells because a change in share does not say where a country started: a gain of 30 points could be a rise from 5% to 35% or from 60% to 90%. Showing both endpoints on a shared 0–100% scale reveals the starting level, and the connecting line gives the size and direction of the change. The lines join two endpoints and do not show annual trends; the next plot shows them.

## Clean Share Over Time

The dumbbells join only two points, so they cannot show what happened in between. This line chart plots the clean share of electricity generation for every year from 2000 to 2024, one line per country, so the evolution is visible. We chose lines because the data is a series over time, and a line makes the slope, timing, and reversals of a trend easy to follow. The vertical scale is fixed at 0–100% so lines from different countries are directly comparable, and up to ten selected countries get distinct colors. Only the 108 compared countries can be selected.

Tick the checkboxes to choose which countries are drawn; the chart starts with the three largest increases and the three largest decreases. **Largest Increases** and **Largest Decreases** select the top countries in one click, and the **Countries per Preset** slider sets how many; moving it updates the selection while a preset is active. **Clear** empties the selection. Hover a line or its label to highlight that country and show its name in a tooltip, and hover a point for a tooltip with the country's name, the year, and its clean share that year.

// Load the Country-Year Table Untyped (Blank Shares Stay Unknown, Never Zero), then Keep the Compared Countries
const countryYear = await FileAttachment("data/country_year.csv").csv();
const comparedNames = comparison.map((d) => d.country).sort(d3.ascending);
const trendSeries = new Map(comparedNames.map((name) => [name, []]));

  b.className = "reset-button";
  b.textContent = label;
  b.setAttribute("aria-pressed", "false");

    syncPresetButtons();

  for (const {b, mode} of presetButtons) {
    b.classList.toggle("is-active", mode === presetMode);
    b.setAttribute("aria-pressed", String(mode === presetMode));
  }
}

    .attr("cx", (d) => trendX(d.year))
    .attr("cy", (d) => trendY(d.share))
    .attr("r", 2.5)
    .attr("fill", (d) => trendColor(d.name))
    .append("title")
    .text((d) => `${d.name}, ${d.year}\nClean Share: ${pct(d.share)}%`);

  countryGroups.append("text")
    .attr("x", trendDims.width + 8)
    .attr("y", (d) => labelY.get(d) ?? 0)
    .attr("dy", "0.35em")
    .attr("fill", (d) => trendColor(d))
    .attr("font-weight", 600)
    .text((d) => d);
}

trendPanel.setChart(trendSvg.node());
