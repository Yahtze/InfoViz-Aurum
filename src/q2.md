# Q2: Clean Sources of Electricity Generation by Country, Share and Mix, 2024

**Question:** How do countries compare in their clean electricity share in 2024, which have the highest and lowest shares, and what is each country's electricity generation mix made of?

We explore the question with three plots:

1. **[Clean Share Map](#clean-share-map)** is a choropleth map of every country.
2. **[Highest and Lowest Shares](#highest-and-lowest-shares)** is a pair of ranked horizontal bar charts.
3. **[Electricity Mix by Country](#electricity-mix-by-country)** is a set of donut charts, one per selected country.

## Clean Share Map

Each country is shaded by its clean share of electricity generation in 2024. We chose a choropleth because the question is about comparing countries, and a map shows geographic patterns, such as clusters of high or low shares in a region, that a list cannot. Shares run from 0 to 100%, so a single sequential color scale from light to dark reads as low to high. Countries with no data get a grey hatch pattern, so they are not mistaken for a low share.

**Note:** We adjusted the standard YlGnBu color scale. It starts 15% of the way along the ramp instead of at its near-white end, so a 0% share stays visible against the white page. The shades are therefore slightly darker than the unadjusted scale at every value, but the scale is still linear from 0 to 100%.

**Specific Interactions:** Hover a country for a tooltip with its name, clean share, clean generation in TWh, and total generation in TWh; countries with no data show their name and "No Matched Data". Scroll to zoom and drag to pan the map; **Reset View** restores the original position.

## Highest and Lowest Shares

Countries are ranked by their 2024 clean share of electricity generation. The left panel shows the highest shares and the right panel the lowest. We chose ranked bars because a map is poor at exact comparison: color is hard to read precisely, and small countries are barely visible. Bars on a shared 0–100% axis make exact values easy to compare and put the two extremes side by side, including places too small to see on the map.

**Specific Interactions:** Use the slider to change how many countries each panel shows, and hover a bar for a tooltip with the country's name, clean share, and total generation in TWh.

## Electricity Mix by Country

The map and rankings show only the clean share. This section shows what the rest of each country's electricity is made of in 2024: one donut per selected country, split into the three fossil sources (coal, gas, and oil) and the six clean sources (wind, solar, hydro, nuclear, bioenergy, and other renewables). We chose donuts because the question is about parts of a whole, and one small donut per country lets mixes be compared side by side. The donut hole shows the clean share. Slices follow the same order in every donut, with fossil sources first and clean sources after, so the clean part always lies on the same side. Angles are harder to compare than bar lengths, so exact values are available on hover, and slices under 5% are left unlabeled.

**Specific Interactions:** Tick the checkboxes to choose which countries are drawn; the chart starts with the three highest and the three lowest clean shares. **Highest Shares** and **Lowest Shares** select the top countries in one click, and the **Countries per Preset** slider sets how many; moving it updates the selection while a preset is active. **Clear** empties the selection. Hover a slice for a tooltip with the country's name, the source, and that source's exact share and generation in TWh. Hover a legend item to dim the other sources and highlight that source in every donut, and click it (or press Enter) to keep the highlight; click it again to clear.

  const slices = mixPie(mixSources.map((s) => ({source: s, share: +d[`${s.key}_share_pct`] || 0, twh: +d[`${s.key}_twh`] || 0})));

  const donut = mixSvg.append("g").attr("transform", `translate(${cx}, ${cy})`);

  const arcs = donut.selectAll("g")
    .data(slices.filter((a) => a.data.share > 0))
    .join("g")
    .attr("class", "mix-slice highlightable")
    .attr("data-source", (a) => a.data.source.name);

  arcs.append("path")
    .attr("d", mixArc)
    .attr("fill", (a) => a.data.source.color)
    .attr("stroke", "#ffffff")
    .attr("stroke-width", 1)
    .append("title")
    .text((a) => `${name}\n${a.data.source.name}: ${mixPct(a.data.share)}% (${d3.format(",.1f")(a.data.twh)} TWh)`);

  arcs.filter((a) => a.data.share >= 5)
    .append("text")
    .attr("transform", (a) => `translate(${mixArc.centroid(a)})`)
    .attr("text-anchor", "middle")
    .attr("dy", "0.35em")
    .attr("font-size", 10)
    .attr("fill", (a) => labelFill(a.data.source.color))
    .attr("pointer-events", "none")
    .text((a) => `${Math.round(a.data.share)}%`);

  // Donut Hole: the Clean Share
  donut.append("text")
    .attr("text-anchor", "middle")
    .attr("font-size", 18)
    .attr("font-weight", 700)
    .attr("dy", "-0.1em")
    .text(`${mixPct(d.cleanShare)}%`);

  donut.append("text")
    .attr("text-anchor", "middle")
    .attr("font-size", 10)
    .attr("opacity", 0.7)
    .attr("dy", "1.3em")
    .text("Clean");

  // Country Name and Total Generation
  donut.append("text")
    .attr("y", mixDims.outerRadius + 20)
    .attr("text-anchor", "middle")
    .attr("font-size", 12)
    .attr("font-weight", 600)
    .text(name);

  donut.append("text")
    .attr("y", mixDims.outerRadius + 34)
    .attr("text-anchor", "middle")
    .attr("font-size", 10)
    .attr("opacity", 0.7)
    .text(`${d3.format(",.1f")(d.generationTwh)} TWh Generated`);
});

// Restore a Pinned Highlight after a Redraw
mixHighlight(null);

mixPanel.setChart(mixSvg.node());

## Comparison of the Visualizations

| | Clean Share Map | Highest and Lowest Shares | Electricity Mix by Country |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shows** | Every country's clean share, as a color on a map | The countries with the highest and lowest clean shares, as bar lengths | What each selected country's electricity is made of, as slices of a donut |
| **Strengths** | Geographic patterns are visible, such as clusters of high or low shares in a region, and every country appears at once. | Exact values are easy to compare on a shared 0–100% axis, and the two extremes sit side by side, including places too small to see on the map. | The full mix is visible, not just the clean share, and countries can be compared side by side with the same source order in every donut. |
| **Weaknesses** | Color is hard to read precisely, and small countries are barely visible. Eight places cannot be drawn at all. | Only the extremes are shown, so the middle of the distribution and any geographic pattern are hidden, and the mix behind each share is not shown. | Angles are harder to compare than bar lengths, so exact shares need hover, and only a few countries fit on screen at once. |
| **Best For** | Spotting regional patterns in clean share | Looking up and comparing exact shares at the top and bottom | Understanding what a country's clean and fossil electricity consists of |
